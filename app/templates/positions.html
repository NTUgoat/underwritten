No position is published. A position is opened only after the ledger it rests on is complete, and it is signed and dated when it is. Publishing a stance now, ahead of the evidence, would invert the order this method is built on.

The shape every memo takes

  1. Stance, in the first eight words

    If it cannot be said in eight words it is not yet a position.

  2. Variant perception

    What is believed here that the price does not appear to reflect. Written as an observation about the filed record, never as an allegation.

  3. What would have to be true

    The conditions the position depends on, listed so a reader can price them separately.

  4. Expected return as a spread, in basis points

    Over a cost of capital built line by line from published country and industry risk premia, each line citing its edition, plus a capital-structure adjustment. Never a target price.

  5. Downside stress case

    What the position costs when the thesis is wrong, stated before it is.

  6. Three dated kill criteria

    Machine-checkable against incoming filings, evaluated by a scheduled job without the author's involvement. A killed position stays visible, with the filing that killed it.
